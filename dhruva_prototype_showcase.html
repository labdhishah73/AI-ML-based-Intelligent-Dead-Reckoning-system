What the prototype actually is, and what it has to do on Sept 7

This is the answer to "what are we showing, and what's our end goal" — not the day-by-day build plan (PROTOTYPE_PLAN.md) and not the team roles (dhruva_team_structure.html). This is the content spec for the thing itself.

Section 1The end goal, in one sentence

By end of day, Sept 7

A live app, on a real phone, keeps tracking a moving dot on an offline map through a real GNSS-blocked segment of a real test route — using a genuinely trained speed model, not a scripted animation — plus two written artifacts the problem statement explicitly requires: a position-plot result from the IO-VNBD dataset, and a standalone script proving the same model runs without a phone at all.

Every section below exists to make that one sentence concrete: what "live," "real," and "genuinely trained" actually mean, what gets shown in what order, and what still counts as success if part of it doesn't fully work.

Section 2What "prototype" means here

This is a proof of the architecture's central claim, not the full production system described in DHRUVA_PROJECT_CONTEXT.md. Several pieces are deliberately simplified so the team can ship something real in three days instead of something impressive-on-paper and untested. Section 5 lists exactly what's simplified and why — none of it is hidden from the demo narrative.

The one claim that must survive intact

GNSS enters only as a correction, never as part of the process model. Dead reckoning runs continuously whether or not satellites are visible. This is the architectural point (see DHRUVA_PROJECT_CONTEXT.md §4) and the prototype exists to make it visible on a screen, not just claim it in a slide.

Section 3The demo, scene by scene

What a judge actually sees, in order. Each scene has a purpose — nothing here is filler.

Scene 1

App opens, offline map loads — phone in airplane mode

Proves the "zero network calls during navigation" claim before anything else happens. If the map loads with no connectivity, the offline-first architecture isn't a slide claim anymore.

Scene 2

Normal walk/drive, GNSS available

The dot follows the real route on the offline map. Confidence circle stays small and tight. This is the baseline "everything working normally" state the rest of the demo will visibly depart from and return to.

Scene 3

Entering the GNSS-blocked segment

GPS signal is cut (underground parking, covered walkway, or a shielded antenna — see PROTOTYPE_PLAN.md §2). The dot keeps moving, driven by the speed model and orientation filter alone. The confidence circle visibly grows. Nothing on screen freezes or jumps — this is the moment that answers "what happens in a tunnel."

Scene 4

Exiting the blocked segment — GNSS reacquisition

The hardest engineering moment in the whole architecture (see DHRUVA_PROJECT_CONTEXT.md §8, "hardest engineering problem"). The first GPS fix after a long gap may disagree with the accumulated estimate — a good filter eases the correction in as the confidence circle shrinks; a bad one snaps the dot visibly. Whichever happens, this is the single most technically interesting thing to narrate live.

Scene 5

Voice guidance continuing underground

Turn-by-turn instructions keep coming during the blackout, because the route and road graph are local. Short, but it's the user-facing payoff of everything above.

Scene 6

The position-plot result (slide, not live)

The naive dead-reckoning baseline plotted against true GPS — already produced on real data (ml/baseline_position_plot.py, run Sept 4) — shown alongside whatever the trained model's own predicted-vs-true speed plot looks like. This is the literal screening deliverable, presented as evidence, not claimed as a number in prose.

Scene 7

The edge-deployable engine, running standalone

The same trained model, on a laptop, with no phone involved: a CSV of external IMU data in, a position plot out. Proves the "same models running standalone for external IMU input" deliverable isn't just a line in the deck.

If the live demo breaks

Switch to the recorded fallback (owned by the Demo & Presentation Lead, recorded Day 2 per PROTOTYPE_PLAN.md). Scenes 6 and 7 don't depend on the live app at all and can carry the technical proof even if Scenes 1–5 have to be shown as a recording instead of live.

Section 7What "success" looks like

Concrete and measurable — not "it works well," but specific things that either happened or didn't.

Success criteria
  • The app runs the full Scene 1→5 sequence live, on a real phone, without crashing.
  • The confidence circle visibly grows during the GNSS-blocked segment and visibly shrinks after reacquisition — eases in, doesn't snap.
  • A real drift percentage exists from the actual field-test route, measured and stated honestly — whatever number it turns out to be.
  • Both written deliverables (position plot, edge-engine script) exist, run, and produce real output on real data.
  • Every technical role can answer their section of the Q&A cheat sheet without contradicting what the demo just showed.

Section 8What we will not claim

Non-negotiable, from DHRUVA_PROJECT_CONTEXT.md §11
  • No unmeasured result stated as fact — if the field test hasn't run yet, the drift number doesn't exist yet either.
  • Say Unscented Kalman Filter only if that's genuinely what's running — the prototype's v1 filter is a simplified Kalman filter, and that distinction gets stated, not blurred.
  • Say GNSS, not "GPS."
  • Exactly two trained models — the speed model, and (if finished in time) the noise-weighting network. Not more, by accident of loose language.
  • Don't claim the <10% drift benchmark is met unless the field test actually measured it.
