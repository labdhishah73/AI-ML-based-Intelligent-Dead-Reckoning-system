DHRUVA — who's building what, and what they need to know cold

Eight roles for eight people, mapped onto the 3-day prototype plan (PROTOTYPE_PLAN.md) and the full architecture (DHRUVA_PROJECT_CONTEXT.md). Every role gets the same depth of detail — including the two labeled "non-technical."

Section 1Why 6+2, not 8 generic roles

The project has six real engineering domains — the six technical roles below map onto them directly. Two more people are needed for documentation and for running the actual demo, and it would be a mistake to treat those as roles anyone can fill with zero technical grounding.

Read this before assigning people

"Non-technical" here is a label, not a skill requirement. The Documentation & Research Lead has to understand the Unscented Kalman Filter well enough to correct a jury's misconception about it. The Demo & Presentation Lead has to recognise, live, whether the confidence circle growing on screen means the filter is working correctly or means something just broke — and know which one it is before a judge asks. Neither role is "the person who doesn't need to get the tech." They're the two roles that have to understand all of it, horizontally, rather than going deep on one piece of it.

Put your two strongest communicators in roles 7 and 8 — not your two least technical people. If someone can't currently explain why GNSS "joins only at the measurement update," they're not ready for either non-tech role yet, and that's a Day 0 problem to fix, not a Day 3 surprise.

Section 2The 8 roles at a glance

  • 1ML Engineer— trains the CNN-LSTM speed model, the one piece that must exist
  • 2Data Engineer— owns the dataset pipeline; already shipped and validated on real data
  • 3Mobile App Engineer— React Native shell, offline map, UI, confidence circle
  • 4Sensor & Fusion Engineer— Madgwick orientation + the simplified Kalman filter
  • 5Integration Engineer— wires the trained model into the live app; the single biggest risk
  • 6Systems / Infra Engineer— OSM tiles, environment setup, the standalone edge-engine script
  • 7Documentation & Research Lead— non-tech label, full technical fluency required — owns the story and the Q&A
  • 8Demo & Presentation Lead— non-tech label, full technical fluency required — owns the room

Section 3How the roles connect

Three tracks start independently and don't block each other. Two of them converge into Integration, which is the highest-risk single point in the whole plan — nothing reaches the phone screen until it works.

Role 1 · Technical coreML Engineer — Speed Model

Technical core

Own the CNN-LSTM speed & vibration model — the one component in the whole architecture that must exist, because it's what replaces the speedometer and satisfies the "no connection to the vehicle" constraint.

What you own

  • The model architecture: Conv1D layers for local vibration patterns → LSTM for temporal consistency → Dense output to scalar velocity.
  • Training against ml/data_loader.py's streaming_dataset() generator.
  • TFLite export with int8 post-training quantization for the phone.

Must know cold

  • Why this is per-window regression, not integration — the model recognises speed from vibration signature per window, so a bad guess in one window doesn't corrupt the next. No accumulating error, unlike double-integrating acceleration.
  • Why the label must come only from the V-file (vehicle ground truth) and never from any GPS/S-file column — the data leakage rule. If this model ever sees real speed at inference or simulated-blackout evaluation, the result is meaningless.
  • What quantization costs and doesn't cost (~4× smaller, faster, <1% accuracy loss typically — verify this on your own model, don't assume it).
  • Why the team chose CNN-LSTM over one end-to-end network for the whole pipeline: not enough data in IO-VNBD for that, wouldn't transfer across sensor grades, and gives no uncertainty estimate for the confidence circle.

Day-by-day

DayTask
Day 0Read ml/schema.py and ml/data_loader.py. Set up TensorFlow. Decide window size and stride with the Data Engineer.
Day 1Build and train a first-pass model against the 72 synchronised Structure-1 file pairs. Produce a loss curve and a predicted-vs-true speed plot.
Day 2Quantize to TFLite int8. Verify accuracy didn't collapse post-quantization. Hand the .tflite file to the Integration Engineer.
Day 3Support integration bugs if the model behaves unexpectedly on-device. Help interpret the field-test drift number honestly.

Deliverables

  • Trained model file + TFLite export.
  • Loss curve and predicted-vs-true speed plot — this doubles as part of the missing screening deliverable.
Depends on / feeds into

Needs clean windowed data from the Data Engineer. Hands the trained model to the Integration Engineer and to the Systems/Infra Engineer (for the standalone edge-engine script).

Role 2 · Technical coreData Engineer — Dataset Pipeline

Technical core

Own everything between the raw IO-VNBD CSVs and clean, safe training windows. This role is already built and validated against real data as of Sept 4.

What you own

  • ml/schema.py — normalizes all 6 smartphone column layouts and the 1 vehicle layout into two canonical schemas.
  • ml/data_loader.py — finds matched S/V file pairs, loads and normalizes them, splits recording sessions, and streams fixed-size windows without ever loading the full 30–40GB set into memory.
  • ml/baseline_position_plot.py — the naive dead-reckoning baseline, already run successfully.

Must know cold

  • The six S-file column structures and why only Structure 1 (Yaw/Pitch/Roll gyro labeling) is used for v1 training — Structures 3/4 use the same column positions for X/Y/Z, and whether those are the same physical axes is unverified.
  • The synchronised vs unsynchronised tree distinction — only the synchronised tree has row-aligned S/V timestamps. The unsynchronised tree needs timestamp interpolation, out of scope for the prototype.
  • The session-reset gotcha found in S-M.csv: TIME SINCE START (ms) jumps backward mid-file because multiple recording sessions are concatenated in one CSV. Any time-based slicing must use row position, never a boolean filter on the raw time value, or a later session's small timestamps alias into an earlier window.
  • CSVs are Latin-1 encoded, not UTF-8 (the m/s² / μT header characters are the tell).
Status

Done and validated: 72 synchronised pairs discovered correctly, schema normalization confirmed correct, real baseline number produced (2184% drift on a 60-second segment — expected and correctly diagnostic, not a bug).

Day-by-day

DayTask
Day 0Done — dataset downloaded, pipeline validated end-to-end.
Day 1Spot-check more files for additional session-reset-style anomalies. Prep the exact windowed dataset object for the ML Engineer's training loop.
Day 2Support the ML Engineer with any data issues surfacing during training.
Day 3Support parsing of field-test recordings if the demo needs its own logged data analyzed.
Depends on / feeds into

Not blocked by anyone. Blocks the ML Engineer — nothing on that track starts without this.

Role 3 · Technical coreMobile App Engineer

Technical core

Own the React Native app shell, the offline map, and the on-screen experience — the part a judge actually watches.

What you own

  • RN project scaffold, screen layout, navigation UI.
  • MapLibre GL integration with offline MBTiles (no live map API, no keys, no connectivity dependency).
  • The confidence circle — sized directly off the fusion filter's own covariance, not a separately invented number.
  • Voice guidance stub reading from the locally stored route.

Must know cold

  • Why React Native over Flutter: the phone only needs 10Hz updates (the 200Hz figure is for the edge engine, not the phone) — at that rate the framework isn't the bottleneck, team velocity is.
  • Why React Native over native Kotlin/Swift: native gives tighter sensor control but costs a full UI rewrite; RN can still drop just the sensor layer into a native module later.
  • Why MapLibre + offline OSM over Google Maps SDK: removes paid keys and the exact connectivity dependency the whole pitch argues against.
  • What MBTiles is (a single file of pre-rendered map tiles read straight from local storage) and why the confidence circle is "more honest than Google Maps" — Google Maps shows a confident dot while guessing; this surfaces real uncertainty.

Day-by-day

DayTask
Day 0Scaffold the RN project. Install MapLibre RN bindings. Help pick the demo area with Infra and Demo Lead.
Day 1Get the offline map rendering on a real device. Get raw sensor streams (accel/gyro/mag/GPS) flowing into JS and logging to console — no fusion yet, just plumbing.
Day 2Wire the fused position stream (from Integration) to the map marker at ~10Hz. Add the confidence circle.
Day 3Field test, feature freeze by midday, rehearsal.
Depends on / feeds into

Needs the fused position stream from Integration Engineer and the map tiles from Infra Engineer. Feeds the finished app to the Demo & Presentation Lead.

Role 4 · Technical coreSensor & Fusion Engineer

Technical core

Own orientation estimation and the position filter — the classical half of the architecture that has to be right for the AI half to mean anything.

What you own

  • Madgwick orientation filter (use a known-good open-source implementation — don't write the quaternion math from scratch under this deadline).
  • A simplified Kalman filter tracking position, velocity, and heading — without IMU bias states in v1 (that's the scoped-down part of the full architecture's UKF).
  • A heading-consistency sanity check standing in for the full non-holonomic pseudo-measurement.

Must know cold

  • The Madgwick trick: gravity tells you which way is down (pitch/roll), hard braking/accelerating tells you which way is forward (PCA over the dominant horizontal acceleration axis) for yaw.
  • Why the full design uses a Kalman filter's predict/update cycle in general, and specifically why the real architecture wants an Unscented Kalman Filter — not "Kalman Filter" — over the older EKF (avoids hand-derived Jacobians on a quaternion state, handles large uncertainty growth during long outages better).
  • Why v1 skips the bias states: they're what let good-GPS driving calibrate the sensor model for the next tunnel, a real capability being deferred, not a hidden flaw pretending not to exist.
  • The correct textbook placement of non-holonomic constraints: as a Kalman pseudo-measurement (zero lateral/vertical velocity in the body frame), not as a separate later stage — even though the prototype implements a simpler post-check version of it.

Day-by-day

DayTask
Day 0Pick a Madgwick implementation. Define the filter's state vector explicitly (position, velocity, heading — no bias terms in v1).
Day 1Implement orientation filtering on canned/logged data. Stub the Kalman filter structure with a placeholder speed input so the interface is fixed early for Integration.
Day 2Wire in the real speed model output and GPS. Add the heading-consistency check.
Day 3Tune against field-test data. Verify the re-acquisition behavior after a simulated GNSS loss eases in rather than snapping.
Depends on / feeds into

Needs the trained speed model's output (via Integration Engineer). Hands the fused position stream to Integration Engineer, which passes it to the Mobile App Engineer.

Role 5 · Technical coreIntegration Engineer

Technical core

Get the trained model actually running inside the live app and streaming through the fusion filter to the map. This is explicitly the single highest-risk task in the whole plan.

What you own

  • The TFLite-in-React-Native binding and native module bridging.
  • The glue code connecting: sensor stream → speed model inference → fusion filter → map update.

Must know cold

  • On-device TFLite runtime constraints — what inference latency is acceptable at a 10Hz target update rate.
  • Basic RN native module bridging, since the sensor layer and the model inference likely both need native-side plumbing.
  • The entire pipeline end to end, better than almost anyone else on the team, because debugging this role's failures means reasoning across all of the other roles' work at once.
Why this role is flagged as the top risk

If this doesn't work, nothing reaches the screen — not because any individual model or filter is wrong, but because the pieces were never proven to work together on a real phone. Start the "hello world" TFLite-on-device test on Day 0, not Day 2.

Day-by-day

DayTask
Day 0Pick a TFLite RN binding library. Run a minimal inference test on the actual target phone before committing to an approach.
Day 1Continue the hello-world integration in parallel with everyone else building their individual pieces.
Day 2Full integration: real model, real fusion, real map update at ~10Hz. Highest-risk day in the entire plan.
Day 3Bug-fix through the field test. Nothing new after feature freeze.
Depends on / feeds into

Needs deliverables from ML Engineer and Sensor & Fusion Engineer. Feeds the Mobile App Engineer. This role's success is the literal success criterion for the demo.

Role 6 · Technical coreSystems / Infra Engineer

Technical core

Own everything around the core pipeline that isn't the model, the filter, or the app screen — including the second required deliverable most teams forget: the edge-deployable engine.

What you own

  • OSM extract for the demo area and MBTiles generation for offline map tiles.
  • Environment and dependency management across the team (ml/requirements.txt, RN setup docs).
  • The standalone edge-deployable engine script: the same trained model, running in plain Python, reading a CSV of external IMU data and producing a position plot — sensor-agnostic by design, no phone required.

Must know cold

  • Why offline OSM/MBTiles matters beyond "no API key": it's proof of the "zero network calls during navigation" claim, which is a core part of the pitch, not a cost-saving detail.
  • What the edge-deployable engine deliverable is actually for: the problem statement requires the same models running standalone for external (non-phone) IMU input, e.g. FOG-grade sensors at 200Hz — this script is the visible proof that requirement isn't being ignored.

Day-by-day

DayTask
Day 0Finalize the demo area and physical route with the Mobile App Engineer and Demo Lead. Clip the OSM extract.
Day 1Generate MBTiles and get them loading in the RN app (paired with Mobile App Engineer). Start the edge-engine script skeleton.
Day 2Finish the edge-engine script using the trained model. Confirm it runs standalone, independent of the phone app entirely.
Day 3Support field-test logistics: recording the fallback video, checking the demo-day environment works without a network connection.
Depends on / feeds into

Needs the trained model from ML Engineer for the edge-engine script. Feeds map tiles to the Mobile App Engineer.

Role 7 · Labeled "non-technical" — full fluency requiredDocumentation & Research Lead

Non-tech label — technical fluency required

Own the story: the project context doc, the Q&A positions, terminology precision, and the deck's technical accuracy. This role has to catch a wrong technical claim before a jury does.

What you own

  • DHRUVA_PROJECT_CONTEXT.md — keeping the open items, decisions, and Q&A positions current as the actual build changes them.
  • The deck's technical accuracy, including the known slide-3 fixes: "Kalman Filter" → "Unscented Kalman Filter", the NHC placement answer, "GNSS Mode / Dead Reckoning Mode" → "GNSS-aided / Inertial only".
  • A one-page Q&A cheat sheet the whole team rehearses from.

Must know cold

  • The full glossary in dhruva_explained.html — every term well enough to define it without notes.
  • Every Q&A position already drafted: "where's the AI, this looks classical" · "why not one big neural network" · "how do you hit the drift benchmark" · "what's your hardest engineering problem" · "what results do you actually have."
  • The communication rules that make or break credibility in the room: never state an unmeasured result, say Unscented Kalman Filter not "Kalman Filter", say GNSS not "GPS", exactly two trained models — not four, not six.
The failure mode this role exists to prevent

A team member improvising a number under pressure — "we get about 95% accuracy" — when nothing has actually been measured yet. This role's job is to have asked every technical role, that same day, what's actually true, so nobody on stage has to guess.

Day-by-day

DayTask
Day 0Read DHRUVA_PROJECT_CONTEXT.md and dhruva_explained.html cover to cover. Start the deck corrections.
Day 1Check in with every technical role on what actually got built that day. Keep the open-items list current. Draft Q&A rehearsal questions.
Day 2Finalize all deck slide corrections. Write out the exact wording for the hardest Q&A answers.
Day 3Run the team through a Q&A grilling session before the live rehearsal.
Depends on / feeds into

Needs a real status update from every technical role, daily — cannot write anything down as fact without asking first. Feeds the Demo & Presentation Lead and the whole team's Q&A readiness.

Role 8 · Labeled "non-technical" — full fluency requiredDemo & Presentation Lead

Non-tech label — technical fluency required

Own the actual demo: the physical route, the GNSS-blocked segment, the recorded fallback, and delivering the pitch in the room. Has to know the difference between "the app is working" and "the app looks like it's working" in real time.

What you own

  • The physical demo route and the GNSS-blocking segment (underground parking, covered walkway, or shielded antenna).
  • The recorded fallback demo, captured personally so its exact behavior is known cold.
  • The feature-freeze cutoff enforcement on Day 3, and the live presentation itself.

Must know cold

  • What the confidence circle growing on screen actually means — filter uncertainty rising during an outage — versus what a genuinely broken app looks like, so a live glitch can be diagnosed instead of just noticed.
  • The exact answer to "what's your hardest engineering problem" (re-acquisition after a long GNSS outage) — because this role is the one most likely to be asked to reproduce that exact moment live.
  • The decision criteria for switching to the fallback recording — decided in advance, not improvised mid-demo.

Day-by-day

DayTask
Day 0Scout and finalize the physical demo route with the Mobile App and Infra Engineers.
Day 1Shadow the Mobile App Engineer's early sensor-streaming tests to build an eye for what "working" looks like before it matters.
Day 2Run the first full field test alongside the Integration Engineer. Personally record the fallback video.
Day 3Run the final field test. Enforce the feature-freeze cutoff. Run the full rehearsal. Present.
Depends on / feeds into

Needs the working app from Mobile App Engineer and the rehearsed answers from Documentation & Research Lead. This role is the last checkpoint before judges see anything at all.

Section 4Shared rules everyone follows, regardless of role

Never break

  • Never state an unmeasured result as fact.
  • Say Unscented Kalman Filter, not "Kalman Filter."
  • Say GNSS, not "GPS."
  • Exactly two trained models — the speed model and the noise-covariance network.

If you're not sure

  • If a term can't survive a follow-up question, don't use it in the deck or on stage.
  • Ask the Documentation & Research Lead before stating anything as an achieved result.
  • Flag scope cuts honestly — "this is architected but simplified for the prototype" is a stronger answer than pretending the cut doesn't exist.
